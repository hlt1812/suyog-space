“
Listening to my body a little more each day.

That's how I describe my own practice, and it's the thread running through everything I write here — yog philosophy, anatomy, breath, and the parts of life that don't separate neatly from any of it.

What I write about

Three threads,
one practice.

Yog philosophy

Where a posture or a breath comes from, and why it's shaped the way it is — the reasoning underneath, not just the how.

Anatomy

How joints, tissue, and habit actually behave — and what that means for the poses everyone's taught to force.

Breath

Pranayama, mostly, but also just noticing — the breath you're holding without realizing it, and what changes once you don't.

Why I write this down: Most of what I've learned about my body came slowly, through repetition and correction, not one clean breakthrough. Writing it down is how I keep paying attention.

New pieces go up as I write them — some structured guides, some shorter notes.

About

Why I started writing this down

I'm Kruti Thakker. Yog philosophy, anatomy, and breath are the three things my own practice keeps circling back to — Suyog.Space is where I put what I'm learning into words, partly so I don't forget it myself, partly for anyone working through the same questions.

I completed my 200-hour Teacher Training Course (TTC), though I think of it less as a finish line and more as where the real learning started — I'm still very much an active student of this practice.

I also write about life more broadly, off the mat, on my personal account.
